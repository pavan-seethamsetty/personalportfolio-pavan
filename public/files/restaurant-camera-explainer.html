    The real case

    Presto launched Presto Vision, a camera system for restaurant lobbies and dining rooms that analyzes how staff and guests move in real time and alerts managers. Outback Steakhouse piloted it at restaurants run by its franchisee Evergreen Restaurant Group, with cameras in the lobby watching how hosts, servers and guests interact. Other vendors now sell similar tools that flag a table guests have left but nobody has cleared.

    The simulation on this page is my own illustration of how such a system works. It is not Presto's actual model, and all the numbers come from the simulation, not from Outback.

    The part I'd watch as a leader

    The same camera that sees a dirty table also sees the person who should have cleared it. Food writers criticized a similar kitchen-camera startup for "spying" on workers. If I were rolling this out, I would tell staff exactly what is recorded, measure tables and wait times instead of ranking individual employees, keep footage only as long as needed, and let a manager, not the model, make any judgment about a person. A model that gets a table wrong costs a wasted trip. A model that gets a person wrong can cost someone their job.
